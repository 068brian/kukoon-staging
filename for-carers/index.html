Let’s talk
Home/For carers

Looking after yourself matters, too.

Caring can be rewarding, funny, frustrating, exhausting and deeply meaningful — sometimes all in the same afternoon.

There isn’t one right way to feel about it.

You might need someone to talk to, a chance to sleep properly, help managing stress, or simply an afternoon when nobody needs anything from you.

Your wellbeing matters independently of your caring responsibilities.

Wellbeing & Connection

Emotional wellbeing and mental health

It’s common for caring responsibilities to affect stress levels, mood and emotional wellbeing. You don’t have to wait until you’re at breaking point to seek support.

  • Carer Gateway (opens in a new tab) — Free counselling, coaching and peer support options for eligible unpaid carers.
  • Beyond Blue (opens in a new tab) — Mental health information and support for anxiety, depression and emotional distress.
  • Medicare Mental Health (opens in a new tab) — Information about free mental health support and services.
  • Lifeline (opens in a new tab) — 24-hour crisis support on 13 11 14.
Physical health, sleep and exhaustion

Caring can be physically demanding. Interrupted sleep, lifting and assisting someone, long hours and limited time for your own healthcare can take a toll.

Your own medical appointments, movement, rest and recovery deserve space in your life.

  • Healthdirect (opens in a new tab) — Reliable Australian health information, including sleep, fatigue and physical wellbeing.
  • Carer Gateway (opens in a new tab) — Explore practical supports and respite that may help you find time to rest.
  • Better Health Channel (opens in a new tab) — Victorian health information covering sleep, exercise, stress and self-care.
Loneliness, friendship and connection

Caring can change your social life. Plans become harder to make, friendships can drift, and sometimes it feels as though everyone else is living a different life.

Connecting with people who understand caring can help. So can spending time with people who know you as someone other than a carer.

  • Carers Victoria (opens in a new tab) — Carer connections, education and community resources.
  • Carer Gateway (opens in a new tab) — Peer support groups and opportunities to connect with other carers.
  • Relationships Australia Victoria (opens in a new tab) — Relationship counselling, family support and education. Fees and eligibility vary.
Relationships, family and boundaries

Caring can change relationships with partners, children, siblings, friends and the person receiving care.

You might find yourself making difficult decisions, navigating disagreements or struggling to balance everyone’s needs.

It’s reasonable to have boundaries. You can care deeply about someone and still need privacy, independence and time for yourself.

  • Relationships Australia Victoria (opens in a new tab) — Counselling and support for relationships and family difficulties.
  • Family Relationships Online (opens in a new tab) — Australian Government information about family relationships, conflict and support services.
  • Carer Gateway (opens in a new tab) — Counselling and coaching relevant to caring responsibilities.
Grief, change and difficult emotions

Not all grief follows a death.

Carers can experience grief when plans change, independence is lost, relationships shift or life looks different from what they imagined.

You can also feel angry, resentful, guilty or relieved. These emotions don’t cancel out your love or commitment.

  • Grief Australia (opens in a new tab) — Grief information, education and support pathways.
  • Beyond Blue (opens in a new tab) — Support for difficult emotions and mental health.
  • Carer Gateway (opens in a new tab) — Counselling and peer connections for unpaid carers.
Finding time for yourself

You are allowed to have interests, ambitions, friendships and plans that have nothing to do with caring.

Sometimes the first step is practical: arranging another person to provide care, finding a respite option or creating a backup plan so you can step away.

  • Carer Gateway — respite (opens in a new tab) — Information about planned and emergency respite options.
  • Carers Victoria (opens in a new tab) — Information and guidance about carer supports.
When things feel unsafe or overwhelming

If caring responsibilities are becoming unmanageable, or you feel unsafe in a relationship or at home, specialist help is available.

  • 1800RESPECT (opens in a new tab) — 24-hour support for domestic, family and sexual violence on 1800 737 732.
  • Safe Steps Victoria (opens in a new tab) — Family violence crisis support in Victoria.
  • Lifeline (opens in a new tab) — Crisis support on 13 11 14.

If someone is in immediate danger, call 000.

A reminder worth keeping

You are more than the things you do for other people.

You deserve care, connection, rest and a life that feels like yours, too.

Money & financial help

Caring can affect income, superannuation, savings and household costs. There may be assistance available, even if you’ve never received a carer payment before.

    Services Australia

  • Victorian Carer Card (opens in a new tab)

    Discounts and benefits for eligible unpaid carers, including participating Victorian businesses.

    Victorian Carer Card

  • Carers Victoria — benefits and concessions (opens in a new tab)

    A directory of payments, discounts, concessions and other assistance.

  • National Debt Helpline (opens in a new tab)
