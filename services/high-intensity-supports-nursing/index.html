Complex care needs. Experienced hands. Individualised support.

When you or someone you love has significant physical care needs, experience matters.

Kukoon provides high intensity disability supports and nursing services for people who require specialised assistance with complex physical health needs.

We understand that these supports can involve significant clinical risks. The right qualifications, practical experience, individual training and clear care procedures are essential.

Experienced, qualified and appropriately trained staff

Complex physical care requires more than general disability support experience.

At Kukoon, we prioritise staff who understand the practical responsibilities of supporting people with significant physical disabilities and complex health requirements.

Our approach includes:

  • Relevant experience

    A minimum of three years’ experience working with people with complex physical care needs.

  • Qualifications appropriate to the role

    Registered nursing qualifications for nursing duties, and relevant qualifications, training and competencies for disability support workers undertaking high intensity activities.

  • Participant-specific training

    Workers must understand the individual’s support procedures, equipment, health risks, communication needs and emergency responses before undertaking the relevant tasks.

  • Competency and safe practice

    High intensity tasks require demonstrated competence appropriate to the support being delivered. Experience alone does not establish competency for a particular procedure.

  • Clear clinical instructions

    Support must follow current individual care plans, prescribed procedures and the directions of appropriately qualified health practitioners.

Our recruitment standard is a minimum of three years’ relevant experience working with people with complex physical care needs, alongside the qualifications and competencies appropriate to each role.

Our focus is on delivering safe, reliable and respectful support that fits around the person’s health requirements, daily routines and preferences.

High Intensity Daily Personal Activities

High Intensity Daily Personal Activities are disability-related supports involving more complex health needs and higher levels of risk.

Depending on individual requirements, clinical assessment, staff competencies and service availability, Kukoon can assess enquiries involving:

  • Complex bowel care — support with individual bowel management routines, including relevant stoma care.
  • Enteral feeding and management — support for participants who receive nutrition, fluids or medication through prescribed enteral feeding arrangements.
  • Severe dysphagia management — support for people with significant swallowing difficulties, following an individual plan developed by appropriately qualified health practitioners.
  • Tracheostomy management — assistance with tracheostomy care and suctioning according to the participant’s clinical requirements.
  • Urinary catheter management — support involving indwelling, intermittent and suprapubic catheter arrangements.
  • Ventilator management — support for participants who depend on assisted ventilation.
  • Subcutaneous injections — support with prescribed injections, including relevant diabetes management.
  • Complex wound management — assistance with wound care requirements and the impact of wound management on daily activities.

Each support is considered individually. Not every task is appropriate for every worker, and some procedures require a registered health practitioner.

Nursing supports

Some participants require nursing care in addition to high intensity disability support.

Where nursing services are clinically indicated and within our available scope, these are delivered by appropriately qualified and registered nurses.

Nursing responsibilities may include clinical assessment, care planning, relevant nursing procedures, participant-specific worker training and review of delegated care arrangements.

The distinction matters: disability support workers can undertake certain high intensity activities when appropriately trained and competent, but they do not replace registered nurses where nursing assessment, clinical judgement or nursing intervention is required.

Care built around the individual

No two participants have exactly the same physical care requirements.

Before commencing a complex support arrangement, we consider the person’s clinical documentation, daily routines, equipment, communication preferences, support environment and relevant risks.

We work with the participant and, with their consent, their family, treating clinicians and other relevant professionals to understand what safe and appropriate support involves.

The aim is to establish clear responsibilities, suitable staffing and practical arrangements that respect both clinical requirements and the participant’s choices.

Supporting transitions from hospital to home

For people with significant physical care needs, leaving hospital or moving into a new support arrangement requires careful coordination.

Where we have the appropriate staffing and clinical capability, Kukoon can work with participants, discharge teams, allied health professionals and support coordinators to assess the requirements for ongoing disability support.

This may involve reviewing care plans, identifying equipment and training requirements, assessing staffing needs and clarifying escalation arrangements before services commence.

A transition can proceed only when the required supports can be delivered safely and suitable arrangements are in place.
